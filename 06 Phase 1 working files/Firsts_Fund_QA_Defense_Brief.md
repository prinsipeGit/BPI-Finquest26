## PART 1 — THE ONE REAL VULNERABILITY

### The conviction scores are hand-assigned. Know this before someone tells you.

The weighting engine is rigorous: weight = conviction ÷ volatility, water-filled against four hard constraints. Volatility is computed from 1,293 real observations. The constraints are verified programmatically.

**But the conviction scores — 88 for ICTSI, 85 for DBS, 70 for Grab — were assigned by judgment, not derived from financial statements.** They are the single largest input to the weights, and they are the softest part of the methodology.

**The question you will get:** *"Walk me through how you arrived at a conviction score of 88 for ICTSI rather than 84."*

**Do not bluff.** A fabricated derivation collapses the moment they probe a sub-score.

**The answer that works:**

> "The score is an investment committee judgment against a published five-factor rubric — Future Relevance 25%, Impact 15%, Resilient Finances 25%, Valuation 20%, Governance 15%. It is deliberately the *discretionary* layer of the process; the mechanical layer is the weighting engine, which converts scores into positions without any further judgment.
>
> The distinction matters: a rules-based fund would compute the score too, and we'd be a quant index. We are an *actively managed* fund. The manager's judgment enters at exactly one point — conviction — and everything downstream of it is deterministic and auditable. That's the design, not a gap in it.
>
> What I can defend precisely is the *sensitivity*. ICTSI at 84 rather than 88 moves its weight from 11.00% to roughly 10.5%, well inside our ±2.5pp rebalancing band. The portfolio is not fragile to the scoring."

**Then pivot to strength:** the Buildout Test is binary and evidence-based, so the *universe* is defensible on hard criteria even where the scoring is judgmental. Jollibee, BDO, SM Prime, Semirara and DMCI are excluded on rules, not opinion.

### Before Phase 2, close this gap

Rebuild each conviction score from actual figures — ROIC, net debt/EBITDA, FCF conversion, EV/EBITDA versus five-year own history, order backlog, CG scorecard. Percentile-rank within the eligible universe rather than assigning absolute numbers. That converts your weakest answer into your strongest.

## PART 2 — THE TWELVE HARDEST QUESTIONS

### 1. "Your excess return is large. Is that stock-picking skill?"
**No — and don't claim it is.** It is principally the geographic allocation decision. The benchmark carries 35% PSEi over a period in which that index declined; we held 30% Philippines and 65% offshore. **The excess return *is* the thesis.** Claiming skill invites them to find the one holding that carried it. Claiming allocation is honest and unfalsifiable.

### 2. "Why is a fund for Filipino youth 65% offshore?"
A 22-year-old's human capital is already a 100% concentrated, unhedgeable bet on the Philippine economy — salary, job security, future rent, future home price, all peso. Building a PSEi-heavy portfolio on top of it doubles down on a risk they cannot diversify away. **We hold 65% offshore because of what they already own, not because of what we think of the country.** BPI Wealth already runs the US Equity Index Feeder Fund, the European Equity Feeder Fund and the ALFM Global Multi-Asset Income Fund.

### 3. "Your back-test is hypothetical. Why should we believe it?"
We don't ask you to. We ask you to check the methodology. Five years including the 2022 drawdown; net of a daily-accrued 1.00% fee; every series converted to PHP; transaction costs, spread and withholding tax explicitly *not* modelled, so real returns would be lower. **And we rejected a more flattering specification:** GE Vernova truncated the panel to 2.3 years and produced roughly 44% with a Sharpe above 2 and no losing 12-month period. We substituted Schneider Electric to obtain a full cycle. We report the harder number.

### 4. "Can your target market actually buy this fund?"
Not under the current CSA — which is the point. A 20-year-old with no emergency fund and no track record scores Conservative and is barred from equity. That framework measures *current* risk capacity and systematically mis-prices a forty-year horizon. We propose a horizon-weighted suitability model plus an education-gated unlock. **The suitability gate is the barrier, and no one is treating it as one.**

### 5. "Isn't this a feeder fund?"
No. A feeder fund holds ≥90% in a single collective investment scheme under Sec. 414 MORB / 414-Q MORNBFI as amended by BSP Circular 1152-22. **We hold 0% in any CIS** — ten directly-purchased listed equities and 5% in direct T-bills and time deposits, deliberately not money market fund units, to avoid fee layering.

### 6. "How is this actively managed if a formula sets the weights?"
The model enforces discipline; the manager exercises judgment. Discretion enters at conviction scoring, a ±3pp deviation band, up to 10% tactical cash, and Investment Committee override on borderline eligibility. **Active share ~93%, beta 0.68, ten holdings against a benchmark of several hundred, and Vertiv and Schneider carry zero benchmark weight.** That is the opposite of closet indexing.

### 7. "Vertiv has 63.7% volatility. In a beginner's fund?"
Which is exactly why it carries the smallest position at 5.40%. Risk budgeting means high-volatility names must earn their capital. Read the weight column against the volatility column — the inverse relationship is the methodology made visible. Portfolio volatility is 12.55%, below several of its constituents.

### 8. "Why should a young Filipino choose you over GCash GFunds at ₱50?"
GFunds offers passive PSEi index exposure, or global exposure through **feeder funds** carrying a second layer of management fees. We offer **direct** global equity selection under an active mandate at 1.00%. And ₱50 access has existed for years while participation fell from 36% to 23% — **price was never the barrier.**

### 9. "Only 30% Philippines. Is that appropriate for a BPI fund?"
Two answers. Fiduciary: see Q2. Factual: the ALFM Growth Fund's five-year annualised return is **1.13% net** against a benchmark of **−0.34%** — BPI's own published figures. A young Filipino who did everything right lost purchasing power. **The manager outperformed by 147 bps; the mandate failed.** That is an argument about portfolio design, sourced from your own disclosure.

### 10. "Grab has never been consistently profitable." ✅ **VERIFIED — PASSES B3**
Grab holds ~**$551M of debt against $7.4B of gross cash liquidity** — it is a **net-cash company**, so there is no leverage to service and the gate passes decisively. FY2025 was its first full year of net profit ($200M, a $358M swing) on adjusted EBITDA of $500M; Q2 2026 delivered revenue of $997M (+22%), adjusted EBITDA of $168M (+54%) and a **$750M buyback announcement.**

**Be ready for the follow-up, because it is a fair one:** Q2 2026 **GAAP operating profit was $19M on $997M of revenue — a 1.9% operating margin.** Most reported profit is finance income on the cash pile, not operations. Concede this cleanly: *"Operations turned positive only recently and the margin is thin. The case rests on ASEAN network scale and a fortress balance sheet, not on current operating profitability. That is why it carries 7.60%, not 12%."*

**If asked why B3's wording changed:** the original text was undefined for net-cash companies — interest cover has no meaning when a company receives more interest than it pays. It now reads *"≤ 4.0× or a net cash position."* **Amending a gate because it was ill-specified is rigour; amending it to admit a name you like is not.** Say that distinction out loud — it will land well.

### 11. "What if the peso strengthens? You're unhedged on 65%."
Unhedged by design. The investor's liabilities, salary and future expenses are 100% peso — the offshore sleeve is the hedge against *their* concentration, not a currency bet. A strengthening peso would reduce returns, and that is disclosed in the risks table. Reviewed semi-annually.

### 12. "Your worst year is −30%. You're selling that to teenagers?"
Yes — before they invest, not after. They are shown −22.18% at onboarding and must acknowledge it. **The fund that hides its drawdown is the one that loses investors when it arrives.** We convert a future shock into a promise already made, and we measure ourselves on redemption behaviour during drawdowns, not on AUM.
