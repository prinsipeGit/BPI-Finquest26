THE CONCEPT
THE PROBLEM EVERYONE IS SOLVING, AND THE ONE THAT ACTUALLY BINDS

Investment participation among Filipino adults fell from 36% in 2021 to 23% in 2025 (BSP Consumer Finance Survey). The industry responded by lowering minimums and digitising onboarding.

That experiment has already been run, and it failed. A Filipino can buy an equity fund on GCash today for ₱50. Sun Life's Peso Starter Fund accepts ₱100. Onboarding is fully digital across the industry. Participation still fell by thirteen percentage points. The minimum was never the binding constraint, which is why lowering it again will not help.

When a 20-year-old opens a Philippine investment app, they fill in a Client Suitability Assessment. It asks about emergency funds, income stability and investment experience: all measures of where they stand today. A first-jobber with none of the three scores Conservative, and every growth fund on the shelf is flagged unsuitable. The ALFM Growth Fund is published for investors "at least classified as aggressive." Nobody is blocked. They are warned, asked to sign a waiver, and told the product they came for is wrong for them.

The binding constraint is not price, and it is not prohibition. It is that the only frictionless path is the wrong one. Sun Life's ₱100 fund is a money market fund. Cheap growth exposure does exist, since GCash lists a PSEi index fund at ₱50, but reaching it means overriding an assessment that has just advised against it. For a first-time investor, defaults decide the outcome. The industry has made capital preservation the path of least resistance for the cohort with the longest runway.

The CSA is not wrong so much as aimed at the wrong target. It measures current risk capacity, which badly mis-prices a 20-year-old. For someone with a forty-year runway and no dependants, the dominant risk is not volatility. It is a lifetime of zero equity exposure. And because every answer is self-reported and unverified, the same gate that steers a candid 20-year-old into a money market fund is cleared in seconds by anyone willing to tick the aggressive boxes. It filters candour, not risk.

THE INSIGHT THAT SHAPES THE PORTFOLIO

A young Filipino's human capital is already a 100% concentrated, unhedgeable bet on the Philippine economy. At 22, the largest asset a person owns is not their portfolio but the present value of their future earnings: peso-denominated, tied to domestic employment, inflation and property prices, and impossible to sell or hedge.

Building a PSEi-heavy portfolio on top of that is not patriotism. It doubles down on a risk they already carry in full, at the age when that concentration is largest relative to net worth. The Firsts Fund holds 30% Philippines and 65% offshore. This is not a verdict on the country. It is the diversification response to what the investor already owns. The Fund earns in hard currency while the investor's salary, rent and future home price stay in pesos.

This is consistent with BPI Wealth's existing shelf, which already includes the BPI US Equity Index Feeder Fund, the BPI European Equity Feeder Fund and the ALFM Global Multi-Asset Income Fund. The Firsts Fund is not a feeder fund: it holds ten directly-purchased listed equities and no units in any collective investment scheme.

What does not exist is a low-minimum route into a growth asset that a young Filipino is actually steered toward. Every cheap product is capital-preservation, passive, or a feeder fund, and every genuine growth mandate sits behind a suitability waiver and share indivisibility.

The most important number on this page is 1.13%: the ALFM Growth Fund's five-year annualised return, net of fees, published by BPI Wealth itself. Its benchmark (90% PSEi + 10% T-bill) returned −0.34%. A young Filipino who cleared the suitability gate and stayed invested for five years in the country's flagship aggressive equity fund still lost purchasing power to inflation. The manager beat its benchmark by 147 bps a year. The mandate failed, not the manager, and that is the case for building the portfolio somewhere else.
WHAT THE FUND OWNS

Ten holdings across five pillars a 19-year-old can name: EARN, MOVE, LIVE, CONNECT, THRIVE. Every holding must clear a binary Buildout Test: at least 20% of revenue or three-year capex tied to a published national or regional capacity target. The gate binds. It rejects Jollibee, BDO and SM Prime on B1, and Semirara and DMCI on conduct. Weights are computed as conviction ÷ volatility, water-filled against hard caps, so no weight is chosen by hand. Equity exposure is then volatility-targeted between 60% and 95%. Over five years including 2022 the portfolio returned 16.18% net of a 1.00% fee, at a Sharpe ratio of 0.87, with a maximum drawdown of −24.94% and a worst rolling twelve months of −22.18%. That last figure is not a footnote. It is the centrepiece of how we onboard.

THE HOOK
THE MESSAGE
“Your first salary should fund more than this month. Fund your firsts, not just your someday.”

Why retirement framing fails. Every existing fund sells a 40-year horizon to someone who cannot picture being 30. Retirement is not a goal for a 20-year-old. It is an abstraction that belongs to their parents. Firsts are vivid, near, and emotionally loaded: the first ₱100,000. The first apartment deposit. The first business. The first time you cover a family emergency without asking anyone.

The Firsts Fund reframes a five-year horizon. Not as "the long term", but as the distance between now and the next version of your life. Same asset allocation, sold against a goal the investor can actually picture.

THE FIVE BARRIERS, AND THE MECHANIC THAT ANSWERS EACH
BarrierWhat it sounds likeOur mechanic
Low income"I don't have spare money to invest." ₱100 minimum, made structurally possible by a unitized fund that transacts to 0.0001 of a unit. Classic mutual fund shares cannot be issued fractionally, so this is a structural choice rather than a marketing one.
Intimidation"This is for people who know things I don't." Five pillars named in plain language. Every statistic carries a one-line explanation. No jargon appears without its translation attached.
Fear"What if I lose everything?" Drawdown pre-commitment. Before investing ₱1 you are shown −22.18%, our worst simulated year, and asked to acknowledge it. We lead with the loss.
Lack of knowledge"I wouldn't know where to start." Education-gated unlock: a 3-minute module that doubles as the compliance pathway for a conservatively-scored investor. Learning is the door, not a detour.
Distrust"Funds are how institutions take your money." We publish the weighting engine. The code that computes every position is open and auditable. Anyone can check that no weight was chosen by hand.
On distrust specifically. This generation grew up watching institutions fail in public. Against that, a brochure promising trustworthiness is worthless. Verifiability beats assurance. Publishing the optimiser ("here is the formula, here are the constraints, run it yourself") is a trust signal no competitor can match with copy.
THE LAUNCH CAMPAIGN: “THE FIRST ₱100”

A single, repeatable, screenshot-native action. Not "start investing", but "post your first ₱100." The campaign asks young Filipinos to name the first they are funding and share the confirmation screen. The unit of virality is a real ₱100 transaction, not a pledge.

DISTRIBUTION ADVANTAGE

Beyond the BPI app, the natural second channel is GCash, which crossed 100 million registered users in early 2026 with an estimated 20–25 million daily active. GCash already operates an investment marketplace distributing third-party funds, and both BPI and GCash's operator sit within the Ayala group, which makes a distribution partnership commercially and structurally realistic rather than aspirational. Placement inside an app young Filipinos already open daily removes the single largest friction in fund distribution: getting them to open a new app at all.

Campaign success metrics: cost per funded account (target <₱350); funded-account conversion from module completion (target >40%); share of accounts opened by investors aged 18–25 (target >70%); organic-to-paid acquisition ratio by month 9 (target >1.0).

ONBOARDING: FIVE STEPS, UNDER TEN MINUTES
StepDesign intent
1e-KYC: ID + liveness selfie Fully digital, no branch visit. Under 5 minutes. Existing BPI clients skip it entirely.
2Horizon-weighted CSA A suitability model that weights time horizon and human capital alongside current financial capacity, correcting a framework that systematically scores a 40-year investor as Conservative.
3Education-gated unlock An investor still scored conservatively completes a 3-minute module on equity risk and time. Academic work on Filipino Gen Z investing finds that perceived behavioural control, meaning literacy and self-efficacy, is the strongest predictor of investment intention. Confidence, not income, is the binding constraint. The module is the intervention.
4Drawdown pre-commitment The investor is shown −22.18%, our worst simulated 12 months, and types the figure to acknowledge it. This converts a future shock into a promise already made, and it is the single most effective retention mechanic in the design.
5First ₱100 + auto-invest Recurring contribution is offered on by default, aligned to payday. Opting out is possible; doing nothing invests.
APPEARING IN THE DAILY ROUTINE

Round-Up Investing

Every card or QR transaction rounds up to the nearest ₱10, sweeping the difference into the Fund. With 58% of Filipino adults transacting on e-wallets and QR now the default in-store rail, the volume is already there. A ₱87 milk tea contributes ₱3. The investor's existing spending becomes the funding mechanism, with no new behaviour and no felt sacrifice.

Payday Auto-Debit

Scheduled to the day salary lands, before discretionary spending begins. Peso-cost averaging by default removes market timing as a decision the investor has to make.

What the Home Screen Shows, and What It Deliberately Does Not

No daily profit and loss. Daily returns are noise at a five-year horizon, and displaying them manufactures exactly the anxiety that drives panic redemption.

Instead: progress toward the named first ("₱12,500 of ₱50,000, First Business"), time invested, and total contributed versus current value. Daily performance stays available, but the investor has to choose to look.

STAYING INVESTED THROUGH A DRAWDOWN: WHERE FUNDS ACTUALLY LOSE PEOPLE
TriggerResponse
Fund falls >10% in 30 days A notification that explains which holdings moved and why, then replays the investor's own pre-commitment: "You said you were ready for −30%. You're at −12%. This is what you signed up for."
Investor opens the redemption screen during a drawdown An interstitial, not a block, showing what redeeming now would have cost in every previous drawdown in the simulation, alongside the 90-day holding period and 1.00% early redemption charge. Then the button. Friction, never obstruction.
Monthly, regardless of market "What moved your money": three plain-language bullets. Builds the habit of reading a market explanation in calm conditions so it is familiar in volatile ones.
MAKING FINANCIAL INFORMATION LEGIBLE TO A BEGINNER

Every metric on the fact sheet carries a plain-language translation in-app. Sharpe ratio 0.75 becomes "for every unit of risk taken, the fund earned 0.75 units of reward above a government T-bill." Maximum drawdown −24.94% becomes "the worst peak-to-trough fall in our five-year simulation." We never remove the technical term. We attach its meaning. The investor who wants to learn the vocabulary can, and the one who doesn't is not excluded.

A2. BACK-TEST METHODOLOGY AND LIMITATIONS stated in full because the result depends on them

Construction. Weight ∝ FIRST conviction score ÷ 3-year annualised volatility, water-filled against caps of 12% per security, 15% per control group, 25% per sector and 30% ±3pp Philippine exposure. Quarterly rebalancing with intra-quarter drift.
Data. 1,293 daily observations, 9 Aug 2021 to 7 Aug 2026, six exchanges, four currencies. All series converted to PHP daily; SGD, MYR and EUR derived as cross-rates through USD. Offshore prices adjusted for dividends and splits. Philippine prices sourced manually, as Yahoo Finance no longer serves individual PSE equities.
Fees. The 1.00% management fee is accrued daily against NAV; reported returns are net, per ALFM convention.
Volatility overlay (C6). Equity exposure is reset monthly to target 12% annualised volatility, floored at 60% and capped at 95%, using trailing 60-day volatility lagged one day so the overlay carries no look-ahead. Average exposure over the window was 74%. It reduced intra-year maximum drawdown in all five calendar years tested, so the effect is a property of the mechanism rather than one fortunate call in 2022.
Not modelled, and therefore overstating returns: transaction costs, bid-ask spread, market impact, withholding tax on foreign dividends, and cash drag from subscription and redemption flows.

Window selection. The five-year window deliberately spans a full cycle including the 2022 drawdown. An earlier specification using GE Vernova, listed only from March 2024, truncated the panel to 2.3 years and produced roughly 44% annualised (gross), a Sharpe above 2, and no negative 12-month period at all. Those figures are an artefact of an exceptionally favourable window for AI-linked equities, not evidence of skill. Schneider Electric was substituted specifically to obtain a full-cycle result. We report the harder number.
Survivorship. Holdings were selected in 2026 and tested on 2021–26 data, so like all back-tests this carries hindsight bias. The result demonstrates that the methodology produces a coherent risk-budgeted portfolio, not that these ten securities were knowable in 2021.
Benchmark selection. An earlier specification used 35% PSEi + 65% MSCI Asia ex-Japan and showed a double-digit annualised excess return. We rejected it: roughly 17% of the portfolio (Schneider Electric, Vertiv) falls outside that universe entirely, and a fund-to-benchmark correlation of 0.65 confirms it did not span the mandate. The policy benchmark mirrors the Fund's own weights (30% PSEi TR + 65% ACWI PHP + 5% T-bill). We report the harder comparison.
Comparison to ALFM Growth is not like-for-like. Our 16.18% is a hypothetical simulation (Aug 2021 to Aug 2026); their 1.13% is actual and realised (Jan 2021 to Jan 2026) with real costs and flows. The legitimate inference concerns mandate design, not relative manager skill.
