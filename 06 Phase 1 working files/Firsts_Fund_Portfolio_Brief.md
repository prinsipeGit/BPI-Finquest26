## 2. The Human Capital Argument (the fund's foundational insight)

**A young Filipino's human capital is already a 100% concentrated, undiversified bet on the Philippine economy.**

At age 22, a person's largest asset is not their portfolio — it is the present value of their future earnings. For a Filipino graduate, that asset is entirely peso-denominated, entirely exposed to domestic employment conditions, domestic inflation, and domestic property prices. It is the single most concentrated position they will ever hold, and they cannot sell it.

Building a PSEi-heavy investment portfolio on top of that is not patriotism. **It is doubling down on a risk they are already maximally exposed to**, at precisely the age when that concentration is largest relative to their net worth.

The Firsts Fund therefore allocates **65% offshore and 30% domestically** — not as a verdict on the Philippines, but as the correct diversification response to what the investor already owns. The fund earns in hard currency while the investor's liabilities, salary, and future expenses are in pesos.

**This is the argument to lead with.** It is fiduciary rather than pessimistic, it is textbook-correct, and it requires no negative commentary on the domestic market.

**Sponsor-fit rebuttal, if challenged:** BPI Wealth already operates the BPI US Equity Index Feeder Fund, the BPI European Equity Feeder Fund, and the ALFM Global Multi-Asset Income Fund. Global allocation is fully consistent with the existing product shelf. The FinQuest primer's own FAQ permits international securities where "the selection and allocation of securities should be clearly justified."

## 3. The Buildout Test (Binary Eligibility Gate)

Eligible **only if all four gates pass.** Failure of any one is disqualifying, regardless of quality.

| Gate | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **B1 — Quantified Buildout** | ≥20% of revenue or forward 3-year capex attributable to an asset base expanding against a *published, externally verifiable* national or regional target |
| **B2 — Liquidity** | 90-day ADTV ≥ PHP 30M (PH) / USD 5M (offshore); free float ≥ 20%; full position liquidatable within 5 trading days at ≤15% of 20-day ADTV |
| **B3 — Solvency** | Net debt / EBITDA ≤ 4.0×, **or a net cash position**; where net debt is positive, EBIT interest cover ≥ 2.5× |
| **B4 — Conduct** | No thermal coal >10% of revenue, no UN Global Compact breach, no unresolved severe controversy, CG scorecard participation or equivalent |

**Worked exclusions — proof the gate binds:** Jollibee (B1 — store rollout is demand-driven, not a published capacity program) · BDO Unibank (B1 — balance-sheet growth is not infrastructure) · SM Prime (B1) · Semirara Mining (B4 — thermal coal) · DMCI Holdings (B4 — consolidated coal) · AyalaLand Logistics (redundant — consolidated within Ayala Land).

## 5. The Weighting Engine

**No weight in this fund is chosen by hand.**

### Step 1–2 — Raw risk-budgeted weight
```
w_raw(i) = ConvictionScore(i) / σ(i)          σ = 3-yr annualised volatility, PHP terms
w(i)     = 0.95 × w_raw(i) / Σ w_raw
```
Conviction earns capital; volatility rations it.

### Step 3 — Constraint stack (iterative water-filling)

| # | Constraint | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Single security | ≤ 12.0% |
| C2 | Single conglomerate / control group | ≤ 15.0% |
| C3 | Single GICS sector | ≤ 25.0% |
| C4 | **Philippine exposure** | **30.0% ± 3.0pp** |
| C5 | Cash & money market buffer | 5.0% |
| C6 | ADTV liquidity cap | ≤15% of 20-day ADTV over 5 days |

### Step 4 — Discretionary Overlay (the "active" in actively managed)

**The quantitative model enforces discipline. The manager exercises judgment within it.**

| Discretion | Band | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Position deviation from model weight | ±3.0 pp | Valuation dislocation, catalyst, or event risk |
| Tactical cash | up to 10.0% | Market stress; Investment Committee sign-off |
| Eligibility override | Committee vote | Documented judgment on borderline B1–B4 cases |
| Off-cycle rebalance | Any time | Material corporate event, thesis break, liquidity deterioration |

All deviations logged with written rationale. Expected annual turnover: **30–50%**.

### Step 5 — Rebalancing
Quarterly, **±2.5pp drift tolerance** per position. Conviction re-scored semi-annually or on material event. B1–B4 breach forces exit within 20 trading days regardless of performance.

### Step 6 — Evidence of Active Management (print on the fact sheet)

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| **Active share vs. benchmark** | **90–95%** |
| Tracking error | 6–10% p.a. |
| Holdings vs. benchmark constituents | 10 vs. several hundred |
| Off-benchmark exposure | Vertiv, GE Vernova — zero benchmark weight |
| Benchmark role | **Measurement only.** No replication, sampling, or tracking |

## 5a. Structural Compliance — Not a Feeder Fund

Under **Section 414 MORB / 414-Q MORNBFI, as amended by BSP Circular No. 1152-22**:

| Structure | Definition | The Firsts Fund |
|---|---|---|
| Feeder fund | ≥90% in a **single** CIS | ✗ **0%** in any CIS |
| Fund-of-funds | ≥90% in **more than one** CIS | ✗ **0%** in any CIS |
| Directly-invested fund | Individual securities selected by the manager | ✓ **95% direct equities, 5% direct T-bills & time deposits** |

The 5% reserve is held as **direct Treasury bills and bank time deposits — not units in a money market fund.** Allocating to another CIS would layer a second fee on the Fund's 1.00% and create a related-party question.

### ✅ B3 verification — Grab Holdings (completed 8 Aug 2026)

**VERDICT: PASSES.** Retained at 7.60%.

| Test | Evidence | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Net debt / EBITDA ≤ 4.0× | Total debt ~**$551M** against gross cash liquidity of **$7.4B** (net cash liquidity $5.4B, FY2025 Q4). **Net debt is negative** — Grab is a net-cash company | **PASS** — no leverage to service |
| EBIT interest cover ≥ 2.5× | Grab earns **net finance income**: interest received on its cash pile exceeds interest paid on debt. The ratio is not meaningful in the usual direction | **PASS on substance** |
| Corroborating | FY2025 first full year of net profit (**$200M**, a $358M swing); adj. EBITDA **$500M**. Q2 2026 revenue **$997M** (+22%), adj. EBITDA **$168M** (+54%), profit **$235M**. Announced a **$750M share buyback** | Balance sheet is exceptionally strong |

**⚠️ The nuance to be ready for.** Q2 2026 **GAAP operating profit was only $19M on $997M of revenue — a 1.9% operating margin.** The bulk of reported profit comes from finance income on the cash pile, not from operations. If challenged on whether Grab is genuinely profitable *as a business*: operations turned positive only recently, the margin is thin, and the investment case rests on ASEAN mobility network scale plus a fortress balance sheet — not on current operating profitability. Do not overstate it.

**Methodology note.** B3 was refined during verification: the original wording ("net debt/EBITDA ≤ 4.0×, EBIT interest cover ≥ 2.5×") produced an undefined result for net-cash companies. It now reads *"≤ 4.0× **or a net cash position**; where net debt is positive, interest cover ≥ 2.5×."* **Disclose this refinement if asked** — amending a gate because it was ill-specified is methodological rigour; amending it to admit a name you like is not, and the distinction is worth stating explicitly.

**On the excess return.** Whatever it restates to, be ready to explain it rather than celebrate it. It is driven principally by the **geographic allocation decision**, not by stock selection: the benchmark carries a 35% PSEi weight over a period in which that index fell, while the Fund held only 30% Philippines and 65% offshore. The excess return *is* the thesis — which is the honest answer, and a much stronger one than implying stock-picking genius.

### What these numbers actually tell you

**The −22.18% worst rolling 12-month is the most valuable figure in this document.** It is the number that goes in front of a 19-year-old at onboarding, before they invest a single peso: *"In the worst year of our simulation you would have been down 30%. If that would make you sell, this fund is not for you."* Concrete, honest, and it converts a future shock into a promise already kept. That single disclosure is the backbone of the drawdown pre-commitment in the CX design.

**84% of rolling 12-month periods were positive — meaning 16% were not.** Quote the second half of that sentence, not the first. It is the honest framing, and it is what makes the drawdown pre-commitment credible rather than decorative.

**Beta of 0.68 with an information ratio of 0.68** is the quantitative case for active management. The fund captured meaningful excess return per unit of active risk while running *less* market beta than its benchmark — the opposite of closet indexing, and evidence that the conviction/volatility weighting is doing real work.

**Correlation of 0.68** confirms the diversification argument in §2: the fund moves substantially independently of the blended benchmark, which is precisely what a young Filipino needs against 100% peso-denominated human capital.

### Methodology disclosures (state these — they pre-empt the obvious attacks)

1. **Window.** Five years including the 2022 drawdown. An earlier draft using GE Vernova truncated the panel to 2.3 years (Mar-2024 onward) and produced a ~44% CAGR with a Sharpe above 2 and *no negative 12-month period whatsoever* — an artefact of an unusually favourable AI-infrastructure window. Schneider Electric was substituted specifically to obtain a full-cycle back-test. **Say this out loud in Phase 2; it demonstrates methodological self-awareness.**
2. **Currency.** All offshore holdings converted to PHP daily. SGD, MYR and EUR rates derived as cross-rates through USD.
3. **Data.** PSE prices sourced manually (Investing.com) as Yahoo Finance no longer serves individual Philippine equities. Offshore prices adjusted for dividends and splits.
4. **Rebalancing.** Quarterly to model weights; positions drift intra-quarter.
5. **Not modelled.** Transaction costs, bid-ask spread, market impact, withholding tax on foreign dividends. Real-world net returns would be lower.

### Three findings from the ALFM Growth Fund fact sheet (30 Jan 2026) — all primary-source

**1. The share-indivisibility trap, quantified.** The fact sheet states plainly: *"Mutual Fund shares do not issue fractional shares"* and *"Transaction amount must be equivalent to at least 1 share."* NAVPS is **₱223.09**. So the smallest possible top-up into the country's flagship aggressive equity fund is **₱223** — a ₱100 contribution is not merely discouraged, it is *structurally impossible*. This is the precise, verifiable justification for our unitized structure, and it is far stronger than asserting "₱100 is more accessible."

**2. The performance case, in BPI's own numbers.** ALFM Growth's **five-year annualised return is 1.13% net of fees.** Its benchmark — 90% PSEi + 10% T-bill — returned **−0.34%** annualised. Frame this carefully: the manager *outperformed* by 147 bps annually and deserves no criticism. **The mandate failed, not the manager.** A young Filipino who cleared the suitability gate, invested, and held for five years still lost purchasing power to inflation. That is the single most persuasive argument for building the portfolio outside the PSEi, and it comes from the sponsor's own disclosure.
