INVESTMENT OBJECTIVE, THEME & THE NEED WE ADDRESS

Objective. The Fund seeks long-term capital appreciation by investing in companies building the infrastructure the next generation of Filipinos is about to use — the power, connectivity, trade, healthcare and enterprise capacity underwriting the first salary, the first home, the first business and the first ₱100,000.

Theme: global thematic — infrastructure buildout. Cross-sectoral and cross-border, spanning energy transition, digital infrastructure, trade, healthcare capacity and digital financial access, expressed through five pillars — EARN · MOVE · LIVE · CONNECT · THRIVE — at 30% Philippines / 65% offshore.

Structure serves the objective. A unitized open-end investment company: fractional units make the ₱100 minimum possible, daily dealing supports peso-cost averaging, and the open-end form lets the manager size positions by conviction and volatility rather than by share indivisibility — which is what makes a risk-budgeted ten-holding portfolio workable at retail scale.

The need. Only 23% of Filipino adults hold any investment, down from 36% in 2021 (BSP). Cheap access already exists and did not fix it. What a young Filipino can reach cheaply, they cannot grow with. ₱50 on GCash buys a local index fund — global exposure there starts at ₱1,000, wrapped in a feeder fund's second fee layer. ₱100 at Sun Life buys a money market fund. Every genuine growth mandate is either priced out of reach or gated behind an Aggressive suitability profile most 18–25s cannot obtain. A young Filipino's human capital is already a 100% concentrated bet on the Philippine economy; 65% offshore is the correct diversification response to what they already own — a household whose savings do not move with its salary is a more resilient household.

CLIENT SUITABILITY

Suitable for investors who:

  • Are classified at least Aggressive under a horizon-weighted risk profile, or have completed the Firsts education-gated unlock;
  • Have an investment horizon of at least five (5) years;
  • Accept that in the worst 12 months of our simulation the Fund fell 30.18%, and will remain invested through it.

Firsts closer than five years do not belong in this Fund. A goal under five years should sit in a money market fund — the ALFM Money Market Fund charges 0.50% and needs only a one-year horizon. The app dates every named first and routes it accordingly. We would rather decline a contribution than mis-place it.

INVESTMENT PROCESS — HOW EVERY HOLDING AND EVERY WEIGHT IS DERIVED

STEP 1 THE BUILDOUT TEST — binary gate; all four must pass:

GateRequirement
B1 Buildout≥20% of revenue or 3-yr capex tied to a published national or regional capacity target
B2 LiquidityADTV ≥ ₱30M (PH) / $5M (offshore); free float ≥20%
B3 SolvencyNet debt/EBITDA ≤4.0× or net cash; where net debt is positive, EBIT interest cover ≥2.5×
B4 ConductNo thermal coal >10% of revenue; no UNGC breach

The gate sets the universe; the engine sets the weights. It is a screen, not a scoring system — it decides what is eligible, not what is owned. Excluded: Semirara and DMCI (B4 — thermal coal); Jollibee and SM Prime (B1 — demand-led expansion is not a published capacity programme). BDO we exclude on the same gate that admits DBS: DBS discloses a multi-year regional technology and platform build serving ASEAN digital payments infrastructure, while BDO's growth is domestic branch and consumer expansion. ALFM Growth holds BDO at 7.2%; we hold neither the view that it is a poor company nor the view that it is a buildout asset.

STEP 2 THE FIRST SCORE — 0–100 conviction:

STEP 3 RISK-BUDGETED WEIGHTING. Weight ∝ FIRST Score ÷ Volatility, water-filled against caps of 12% per security, 15% per conglomerate, 25% per sector and 30% ±3pp Philippine exposure. No weight is chosen by hand. STEP 4 DISCRETIONARY OVERLAY. The manager may deviate ±3pp on valuation or event risk and hold up to 10% tactical cash, with written rationale. Quarterly rebalance, ±2.5pp drift band; expected turnover 30–50%.

How much does the conviction score actually matter? We tested it. Across 120 simulations, each of the ten scores shifted independently by a random amount up to ±10 points, annual return stays within 17.0%–18.5%, maximum drawdown within one point and Sharpe within 0.05. The conviction layer sets the ordering; volatility and the constraint stack do the work. Known refinement: the 12% cap currently binds on DBS and IHH, our two lowest-volatility holdings — a cap that constrains the diversifiers hardest is a design we intend to revisit, not defend.

Weight is capital. Risk is what that capital actually costs you. We publish both because a fund claiming to budget risk should be judged on the second column. Sea, Grab, TSMC, Schneider and Vertiv use 43% of the capital and 70% of the risk; DBS and IHH use 25% of the capital and 9% of the risk. Decomposed into independent factors, 70.3% of portfolio variance sits in one global growth factor and 20.2% in a Philippine factor — ten holdings, two real bets. That is not hidden concentration; it is the 30/65 split doing its job, and it is why the two factors correlate at just 0.03 and why ten names averaging 35.4% volatility produce a 16.8% portfolio.
