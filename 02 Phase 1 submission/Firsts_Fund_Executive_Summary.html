THE CONCEPT
THE PROBLEM EVERYONE IS SOLVING — AND THE ONE THAT ACTUALLY BINDS

Investment participation among Filipino adults fell from 36% in 2021 to 23% in 2025 (BSP Consumer Finance Survey). The industry's response has been to lower minimums and digitise onboarding.

That experiment has already been run, and it failed. A Filipino can today buy an equity fund on GCash for ₱50. Sun Life's Peso Starter Fund accepts ₱100. Onboarding is fully digital across the industry. And participation still fell by thirteen percentage points. The minimum was never the binding constraint — which is why lowering it again would not work either.

Consider what happens when a 20-year-old opens any Philippine investment app today. They complete a Client Suitability Assessment. It asks about emergency funds, income stability, investment experience and stated horizon. Having none of the first three, they score Conservative — and are therefore barred from equity funds, which are sold only to investors profiled Aggressive. The ALFM Growth Fund is explicit: suitable for investors "at least classified as aggressive."

The binding constraint on youth investing is not price. It is the suitability gate. Cheap access already exists — but only into products a conservative profile permits. Sun Life's ₱100 fund is a money market fund, formerly named the Sun Life Prosperity Money Market Fund, explicitly for conservative investors on a short horizon. A young Filipino can start investing for ₱100 today. They simply cannot reach a growth asset at that price without being profiled out of it.

The CSA is not wrong; it is measuring the wrong thing. It assesses current risk capacity, which systematically mis-prices a 20-year-old. For an investor with a forty-year runway and no dependants, the dominant risk is not volatility — it is a lifetime of zero equity exposure. A framework designed to protect young Filipinos is excluding them, and the participation data shows the result.

THE INSIGHT THAT SHAPES THE PORTFOLIO

A young Filipino's human capital is already a 100% concentrated, unhedgeable bet on the Philippine economy. At 22, the largest asset a person owns is not their portfolio — it is the present value of their future earnings. For a Filipino graduate that asset is entirely peso-denominated and entirely exposed to domestic employment conditions, domestic inflation and domestic property prices. It cannot be sold, diversified or hedged.

Building a PSEi-heavy portfolio on top of that is not patriotism. It is doubling down on a risk they are already maximally exposed to, at exactly the age when that concentration is largest relative to net worth. The Firsts Fund therefore holds 30% Philippines and 65% offshore — not as a verdict on the country, but as the correct diversification response to what the investor already owns. The Fund earns in hard currency while the investor's salary, rent and future home price are in pesos.

This is consistent with BPI Wealth's existing shelf, which already includes the BPI US Equity Index Feeder Fund, the BPI European Equity Feeder Fund and the ALFM Global Multi-Asset Income Fund. The Firsts Fund is not a feeder fund: it holds ten directly-purchased listed equities and zero units in any collective investment scheme.

What does not exist is a low-minimum route into a growth asset a young Filipino is permitted to buy. Every cheap product is capital-preservation, passive, or a feeder fund; every genuine growth mandate is gated by suitability and by share indivisibility.

The most important number on this page is 1.13%. That is the ALFM Growth Fund's five-year annualised return, net of fees, published by BPI Wealth itself. Its benchmark — 90% PSEi + 10% T-bill — returned −0.34% annualised over the same five years. A young Filipino who did everything right, cleared the suitability gate and stayed invested for five years in the country's flagship aggressive equity fund lost purchasing power to inflation. This is not a criticism of the manager; the fund beat its benchmark by 147 basis points annually. It is an indictment of a PSEi-concentrated mandate — and it is the strongest possible evidence for building the portfolio somewhere else.
WHAT THE FUND OWNS — AND WHY A YOUNG INVESTOR CAN UNDERSTAND IT

Ten holdings, organised into five pillars a 19-year-old can name: EARN (work and enterprise), MOVE (trade and mobility), LIVE (power and home), CONNECT (digital and financial access) and THRIVE (health and essentials). Every holding must clear a binary Buildout Test: at least 20% of revenue or three-year capex tied to a published national or regional capacity target. The gate binds — it rejects Jollibee, BDO and SM Prime on B1, and Semirara and DMCI on conduct.

Weights are then computed as conviction score ÷ volatility, water-filled against hard caps. No weight in this Fund is chosen by hand. Over a five-year simulation including the 2022 drawdown, the resulting portfolio returned 17.98% annualised net of fees against a benchmark return of 7.52%, with a maximum drawdown of −33.25% and a worst rolling twelve months of −30.22%. That last figure is not buried in a footnote. It is the centrepiece of how we onboard investors.

THE HOOK
THE MESSAGE
“Your first salary should fund more than this month. Fund your firsts — not just your someday.”

Why retirement framing fails. Every existing fund sells a 40-year horizon to someone who cannot picture being 30. Retirement is not a goal for a 20-year-old; it is an abstraction belonging to their parents. But firsts are vivid, near, and emotionally loaded: the first ₱100,000. The first apartment deposit. The first business. The first time you cover a family emergency without asking anyone.

The Firsts Fund reframes a five-year horizon not as "the long term" but as the distance between now and the next version of your life. That is the same asset allocation, sold against a goal the investor can actually see.

THE FIVE BARRIERS — AND THE SPECIFIC MECHANIC THAT ANSWERS EACH
BarrierWhat it sounds likeOur mechanic
Low income"I don't have spare money to invest." ₱100 minimum, made structurally possible by a unitized fund transacting to 0.0001 of a unit. Classic mutual fund shares cannot be issued fractionally — this is a structural choice, not a marketing one.
Intimidation"This is for people who know things I don't." Five pillars named in plain language. Every statistic carries a one-line explanation. No jargon appears without its translation attached.
Fear"What if I lose everything?" Drawdown pre-commitment. Before investing ₱1 you are shown −30.22% — our worst simulated year — and must acknowledge it. We lead with the loss.
Lack of knowledge"I wouldn't know where to start." Education-gated unlock: a 3-minute module that also serves as the compliance pathway for a conservatively-scored investor. Learning is the door, not a detour.
Distrust"Funds are how institutions take your money." We publish the weighting engine. The code that computes every position is open and auditable. Anyone can verify that no weight was chosen by hand.
On distrust specifically. This generation grew up watching institutions fail publicly. Against that, a brochure promising trustworthiness is worthless. Verifiability beats assurance. Publishing the optimiser — "here is the formula, here are the constraints, run it yourself" — is a trust signal no competitor can match with copy.
THE LAUNCH CAMPAIGN — “THE FIRST ₱100”

A single, repeatable, screenshot-native action. Not "start investing" — "post your first ₱100." The campaign asks young Filipinos to name the first they are funding and share the confirmation screen. The unit of virality is a real ₱100 transaction, not a pledge.

DISTRIBUTION ADVANTAGE

Beyond the BPI app, the natural second channel is GCash, which crossed 100 million registered users in early 2026 with an estimated 20–25 million daily active. GCash already operates an investment marketplace distributing third-party funds, and both BPI and GCash's operator sit within the Ayala group — making a distribution partnership commercially and structurally realistic rather than aspirational. Placement inside an app young Filipinos already open daily removes the single largest friction in fund distribution: getting them to open a new app at all.

Campaign success metrics: cost per funded account (target <₱350); funded-account conversion from module completion (target >40%); share of accounts opened by investors aged 18–25 (target >70%); organic-to-paid acquisition ratio by month 9 (target >1.0).

ONBOARDING — FIVE STEPS, UNDER TEN MINUTES
StepDesign intent
1e-KYC — ID + liveness selfie Fully digital, no branch visit. Under 5 minutes. Existing BPI clients skip entirely.
2Horizon-weighted CSA A suitability model that weights time horizon and human capital alongside current financial capacity — correcting a framework that systematically scores a 40-year investor as Conservative.
3Education-gated unlock An investor still scored conservatively completes a 3-minute module on equity risk and time. Academic work on Filipino Gen Z investing finds perceived behavioural control — literacy and self-efficacy — is the strongest predictor of investment intention. Confidence, not income, is the binding constraint. The module is the intervention.
4Drawdown pre-commitment The investor is shown −30.22%, our worst simulated 12 months, and types the figure to acknowledge it. This converts a future shock into a promise already made — and it is the single most effective retention mechanic in the design.
5First ₱100 + auto-invest Recurring contribution is offered on by default, aligned to payday. Opting out is possible; doing nothing invests.
APPEARING IN THE DAILY ROUTINE

Round-Up Investing

Every card or QR transaction rounds up to the nearest ₱10, sweeping the difference into the Fund. With 58% of Filipino adults transacting on e-wallets and QR now the default in-store rail, the volume is already there. A ₱87 milk tea contributes ₱3. The investor's existing spending becomes the funding mechanism, requiring no new behaviour and no felt sacrifice.

Payday Auto-Debit

Scheduled to the day salary lands, before discretionary spending begins. Peso-cost averaging by default removes market timing as a decision the investor has to make.

What the Home Screen Shows — and Deliberately Does Not

No daily profit and loss. Daily returns are noise at a five-year horizon, and displaying them manufactures exactly the anxiety that drives panic redemption.

Instead: progress toward the named first ("₱12,500 of ₱50,000 — First Business"), time invested, and total contributed versus current value. Daily performance remains available, but the investor must choose to look.

STAYING INVESTED THROUGH A DRAWDOWN — WHERE FUNDS ACTUALLY LOSE PEOPLE
TriggerResponse
Fund falls >10% in 30 days A notification that explains which holdings moved and why, then replays the investor's own pre-commitment: "You said you were ready for −30%. You're at −12%. This is what you signed up for."
Investor opens the redemption screen during a drawdown An interstitial — not a block — showing what redeeming now would have cost in every previous drawdown in the simulation, and the 90-day holding period plus 1.00% early redemption charge. Then the button. Friction, never obstruction.
Monthly, regardless of market "What moved your money" — three plain-language bullets. Builds the habit of reading a market explanation in calm conditions so it is familiar in volatile ones.
MAKING FINANCIAL INFORMATION LEGIBLE TO A BEGINNER

Every metric on the fact sheet carries a plain-language translation in-app. Sharpe ratio 0.77 becomes "for every unit of risk taken, the fund earned 0.77 units of reward above a government T-bill." Maximum drawdown −33.25% becomes "the worst peak-to-trough fall in our five-year simulation." We never remove the technical term — we attach its meaning. The investor who wants to learn the vocabulary can; the one who doesn't is not excluded.

A2. BACK-TEST METHODOLOGY & LIMITATIONS — stated in full because the result depends on them

Construction. Weight ∝ FIRST conviction score ÷ 3-year annualised volatility, water-filled against caps of 12% per security, 15% per control group, 25% per sector and 30% ±3pp Philippine exposure. Quarterly rebalancing to model weights with intra-quarter drift.

Data. 1,293 daily observations, 10 Aug 2021 – 7 Aug 2026, across six exchanges and four currencies. All series converted to PHP daily; SGD, MYR and EUR derived as cross-rates through USD. Offshore prices adjusted for dividends and splits. Philippine prices sourced manually as Yahoo Finance no longer serves individual PSE equities.

Fees. The 1.50% management fee is accrued daily against NAV. Reported returns are net, consistent with ALFM fact sheet convention.

Not modelled — and therefore overstating returns: transaction costs, bid-ask spread, market impact, withholding tax on foreign dividends, and cash drag from subscription and redemption flows.

Window selection. The five-year window deliberately spans a full cycle including the 2022 drawdown. An earlier specification using GE Vernova — listed only from March 2024 — truncated the panel to 2.3 years and produced a 44.25% annualised return (gross), a Sharpe of 2.09, and no negative 12-month period whatsoever. Those figures are an artefact of an exceptionally favourable window for AI-linked equities, not evidence of skill. Schneider Electric was substituted specifically to obtain a full-cycle result. We report the harder number.

Survivorship. Holdings were selected in 2026 and tested on 2021–26 data. Like all back-tests this carries hindsight bias; the result demonstrates that the methodology produces a coherent risk-budgeted portfolio, not that these ten securities were knowable in 2021.

Comparison to the ALFM Growth Fund is not like-for-like. Our 17.98% is a hypothetical simulation over Aug 2021 – Aug 2026. Their 1.13% is an actual, realised return over Jan 2021 – Jan 2026, achieved with real capital, real transaction costs and real subscription and redemption flows. Different periods, different mandates, simulated versus realised. We state this wherever the comparison appears. The legitimate inference is about mandate design — a PSEi-concentrated portfolio faced a benchmark that returned −0.34% annualised — not about relative manager skill.
