## THE HUNT: what the fund owns

### Slide 2 · Where the money goes
*Speaker 2 · 0:45*

> Opening an investment account has never been easier. What's still hard is knowing where money for a goal five years away should go.
>
> Most choices sit at two ends. On one end, money market funds: steady, but they barely keep up with prices. On the other, short-term trading that most people shouldn't be doing with goal money.
>
> And many local options tie savings even more closely to the same economy that a young Filipino's salary and goals already depend on. Over the last five years, ₱100 in the PSEi became about ₱92.

**Slide:** Two ends of a line: **Park it** (money market) and **Trade it** (speculation), with an empty middle marked **Five years or more?** · Small strip: *PSEi, 5 years: ₱100 → ₱92*

### Slide 3 · What we own
*Speaker 2 · 0:55*

> So we're proposing Firsts Fund: an actively managed global equity UITF that invests directly in stocks, with a recommended horizon of five years or more.
>
> Our theme is one idea: **businesses that own, operate or supply essential capacity that is hard to replace, and earn from providing it.** Power grids. Water systems. Telecom networks. Ports. Airports. Hospitals. And the companies that make the grid equipment and chips that capacity needs.
>
> These are hard to replace because they take licences, decades and enormous capital to build. But essential doesn't automatically mean a good investment. That's why every company has to earn its place.

**Slide:** Headline: *We own what everyday life runs on* · Eight icons: Power & grids · Water · Digital networks · Ports · Airports & toll roads · Hospitals · Grid equipment · Semiconductors · Small line: *Research universe, not fixed allocations*

### Slide 4 · How we choose: Map, Verify, Position
*Speaker 3 · 1:05*

> Every company goes through three steps, and we wrote the rules down before running any results.
>
> **Map the milestone.** Does the company really provide essential capacity, and does it earn from it? At least half its revenue has to come from that capacity.
>
> **Verify the investment.** Is it financially strong, at a fair price? We stress-test its debt with a shock sized to its own industry's worst real episode. Then we judge its value and quality against its own peers, not one universal cutoff.
>
> **Position the risk.** What does it add to the portfolio? We size holdings so no single stock dominates the fund's risk, and we compare that against simple equal weights.
>
> We started with 140 companies. 129 passed Map. 49 passed Verify. **31 made the fund**, across 14 markets. That number came out of the process. We didn't set it. The Philippines is 4.8% through ICTSI, eligible under the same rules as everyone else, with no quota.

**Slide:** Funnel: **140 → 129 → 49 → 31** · **M** Map the milestone · **V** Verify the investment · **P** Position the risk · Small line: *Rules fixed 8 Oct 2026, before any results were run*

> **ICTSI**, the Manila-based global port operator. Its home terminal concession was renewed for another 25 years in 2026. It earns well above its cost of capital, and its debt is modest. The main risks are politics and currencies in the emerging markets where it operates. It holds 4.8% of the fund. Think of it as part of someone's first business: the goods coming in and going out.

It trades cheaper than its hospital peers and carries very little debt. The risk is weaker medical-tourism demand and rising staff costs. It holds 4.5%: the hospital beds a family relies on.

## THE HOOK: how people start

### Slide 7 · Name your first
*Speaker 4 · 1:40*

> How do we reach that graduate? Not with retirement. It's forty years away. We use the first.
>
> They'll find us through short videos, campus financial-literacy sessions and workplace talks for new hires. Every one of those leads to the same place in the BPI App, a proposed new feature: **Name your first.** Pick a suggested goal, write your own, or tap "Help me choose."
>
> Then the calculator. Enter the amount and the date. It shows what your contributions alone would add up to, and separately, a range of investment outcomes, good and bad, with every assumption visible. You choose whether the target is in today's pesos or adjusted for inflation.
>
> No raffles, no cash rewards. Research on young Filipino investors found that feeling competent matters most, so the hook is clarity, not hype.

## THE HABIT: how people stay (the one-slide customer journey)

### Slide 8 · Your first, from start to finish
*Speaker 4 · 2:20*

> Starting is half the job. Staying is the rest. So here's the whole journey in eight stages, each built around the question the investor is actually asking.
>
> **One, Discover your first.** "I have so many plans. Where do I begin?" The graduate story reaches them through short videos, creators, campus activities and workplace sessions, and it ends with one action: name your first.
>
> **Two, Name your first.** "What am I working toward?" One goal, an estimated cost, and a date at least five years away. Undecided? Tap "Help me choose my first."
>
> **Three, Plan your first.** "What can I afford?" The calculator compares contributions alone with an illustrative investment scenario, with every assumption visible. Suitability is checked before going further.
>
> **Four, Take the first step.** "How do I begin?" Open the account, choose the funding account and schedule, and authorize. Here we explain the optional account-level glidepath, and investors who enroll give standing authorization.
>
> **Five, Stay with your first.** "How is my plan progressing?" Scheduled contributions, top-ups whenever they like, and a dashboard that shows what they've put in, what it's worth, the gain or loss, their progress and their next contribution.
>
> **Six, Prepare for your first.** "How do I get ready to use this money?" As the date nears, they review the amount and timing. For enrolled investors, the platform gradually redeems Firsts Fund units and invests the proceeds in a lower-risk fund. The fund doesn't change. Their mix does.
>
> **Seven, Fulfil your first.** "Am I ready?" They see what's available and redeem when they need it, allowing for settlement. If it falls short or plans change, they revise. The date never forces a withdrawal or assumes success.
>
> **And eight, Name your next first.** "What comes next?" Same account, a new goal and a new plan, with suitability checked again for the new timeline.
>
> That's the habit: not a single sale, but a loop that brings them back for the next first.
