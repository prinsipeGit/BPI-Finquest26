## THE HUNT

### Slide 2 · Where the money goes
*Speaker 1 · 0:40–1:25*

> Opening an investment account has never been easier. What's still hard is knowing where money for a goal five years away should go.
>
> Most choices sit at two ends. On one end, money market funds: steady, but they barely keep up with prices. On the other, short-term trading that most people shouldn't be doing with goal money.
>
> And many local options tie savings even more closely to the same economy that a young Filipino's salary and goals already depend on. Over the last five years, ₱100 in the PSEi became about ₱92.

### Slide 3 · What we own
*Speaker 2 · 1:25–2:25*

> So we're proposing Firsts Fund: an actively managed global equity UITF that invests directly in stocks. It's for suitable investors with a goal five years or more away, with a proposed ₱100 to start and a 1.5% annual fee.
>
> Our theme is one idea: **businesses that own, operate or supply essential capacity that is hard to replace, and earn from providing it.** Power grids. Water systems. Telecom networks. Ports. Airports. Hospitals. And the companies that make the grid equipment and chips that capacity needs.
>
> They're hard to replace because they take licences, decades and enormous capital to build. But essential doesn't automatically mean a good investment. So every company has to earn its place.

**Slide:** *We own what everyday life runs on* · Eight icons: Power & grids · Water · Digital networks · Ports · Airports & toll roads · Hospitals · Grid equipment · Semiconductors · Key facts strip (labelled *proposed*): Global equity UITF · Direct stocks · 5+ years · ₱100 minimum · 1.50% a year

### Slide 4 · Map, Verify, Position
*Speaker 2 · 2:25–3:35*

> Every company goes through three steps, and we wrote the rules down before running any results.
>
> **Map the milestone.** Does the company really provide essential capacity, and does it earn from it? At least half its revenue has to come from that capacity.
>
> **Verify the investment.** Is it financially strong, at a fair price? We stress-test its debt with a shock sized to its own industry's worst real episode. Then we judge its value and quality against its own peers, not one universal cutoff.
>
> **Position the risk.** What does it add to the portfolio? We size holdings so no single stock dominates the fund's risk, and we compare that against simple equal weights.
>
> We started with 140 companies. 129 passed Map. 49 passed Verify. **31 made the fund**, across 14 markets. We didn't pick that number; the process did. The Philippines is 4.8% through ICTSI, eligible under the same rules as everyone else, with no quota.

**Slide:** Funnel **140 → 129 → 49 → 31** · **M** Map the milestone · **V** Verify the investment · **P** Position the risk · *Rules fixed 8 Oct 2026, before any results were run*

## THE HOOK

### Slide 7 · Name your first
*Speaker 4 · 5:25–7:00*

> How do we reach that graduate? Not with retirement. It's forty years away. We use the first.
>
> Our campaign film tells that graduate's story: many dreams, then choosing one. People find it through short videos, campus financial-literacy sessions and workplace talks for new hires. Every one leads to the same place in the BPI App, a proposed new feature: **Name your first.** Pick a suggested goal, write your own, or tap "Help me choose my first."
>
> Then the calculator. Enter the amount and the date. It shows what your contributions alone would add up to and, separately, an illustrative investment scenario, with every assumption visible. You choose whether the target is in today's pesos or adjusted for inflation.
>
> No raffles, no cash rewards. Research on young Filipino investors found that feeling competent matters most, so our hook is clarity, not hype.

## THE HABIT

### Slide 8 · Your first, from start to finish (one-slide customer journey)
*Speaker 4 · 7:00–9:00*

> Starting is half the job. Staying is the rest. This is our whole customer journey on one slide: eight stages, each answering the question the investor is actually asking.
>
> You've just seen the first three: **Discover** your first, **Name** your first, and **Plan** your first, where suitability is checked before anything is committed.
>
> **Four, Take the first step.** "How do I begin?" They open the account, choose a funding account and schedule, and authorize their investments. This is also where we explain the optional account-level glidepath, and investors who enroll give standing authorization.
>
> **Five, Stay with your first.** "How is my plan progressing?" Scheduled contributions, top-ups whenever they like, and a dashboard that keeps what they've put in separate from what it's worth, along with gain or loss, goal progress and the next contribution.
>
> **Six, Prepare for your first.** "How do I get ready to use this money?" As the date nears, they review the amount and timing. For enrolled investors, the platform gradually redeems Firsts Fund units and invests the proceeds in a lower-risk fund. The fund doesn't change. Their mix does.
>
> **Seven, Fulfil your first.** "Am I ready?" They redeem when they need the money, allowing for settlement. If it falls short or plans change, they revise. The date never forces a withdrawal, and it never assumes success.
>
> **And eight, Name your next first.** "What comes next?" Same account, a new goal and a new plan, with suitability checked again.
>
> That's the habit: not a single sale, but a loop that brings them back for the next first.
