## 2 · Research: what's missing (1:30–2:40)

Before we built anything, we asked one question: where does a young Filipino put money for a first that's five years away?

Access isn't the problem anymore. BPI cut its UITF minimum from ₱10,000 to ₱1,000 in 2023. PSE accounts grew by about half in a single year, and a quarter of them now belong to 18-to-29-year-olds. Young Filipinos are showing up.

**[Slide: the shelf. Four products, one line each.]**

But look at what's on the shelf. Pag-IBIG MP2 is safe, but it lends your savings to the government. Money-market funds are built for the short term. A Philippine index fund buys more of the same economy you already earn your salary in: over the past five years, ₱100 in the PSEi became about ₱92. And global funds mostly own other funds: two layers of fees, and you can't see what you actually own.

✂ And the appetite for growth? A lot of it went to crypto. Nearly half of Filipino crypto owners are 18 to 34.

So what's missing isn't access. It's a product built for a purpose: a long-term first, with global reach, in pesos, that you can actually understand.

That's the gap the Firsts Fund fills.

> Sources to show on the slide: BPI Wealth (2023 minimum change); PSE investor profile 2024 (via Manila Bulletin, 2025); Pag-IBIG; BPI Global Equity FoF KIIDS (29 May 2026); PSEi from PSE Edge (FMETF, `map/psei.json`); Triple-A (crypto). **Re-check each figure against its source before Saturday.**

## 3 · The fund in one breath (2:40–3:10)

The Firsts Fund is a proposed, actively managed global equity UITF, in pesos. It invests directly in 31 businesses providing essential services and infrastructure around the world: power and water, phone and internet networks, ports, airports, hospitals, and the equipment they run on.

₱100 to start. A 1.50% fee, one layer. For goals five years or more away. And every company in it has a name you can look up.

## 4 · How we pick, and how it runs (3:10–5:30)

How do we choose? Three steps, the same rules for every company. We call it M·V·P.

**[Funnel slide: 140 → 129 → 49 → 31, building one step at a time.]**

**Map the milestone.** We start with 140 of the largest listed businesses across eight types of essential service. 129 actually earn their money from it.

**Verify the investment.** We stress-test every company's debt: if its earnings fell the way its industry's did in bad times, could it still pay its interest? Then we compare its value and quality against its peers. 49 pass.

**Position the risk.** We size each holding so no single company dominates the fund's ups and downs. 31 make it in.

Two examples. **ICTSI**, the Manila port operator, is in at 4.8%. Not because it's Filipino, but because it passed the same tests as everyone else. **Manila Water** didn't make it. Only 28% of its operating earnings turn into cash, so it waits on our watchlist. No favourites.

✂ And NVIDIA? It's in, but at just 1.6%, because its price swings so much. The rules set the size, not the headlines.

**[Growth chart: ₱100 → ₱306 vs ₱161. Label: "Today's holdings, backtested. Not actual fund performance."]**

How has this portfolio done? Here's the honest version. Put ₱100 into today's 31 holdings five years ago and, after every cost, you'd have about ₱306. Our benchmark, a global infrastructure fund, turned ₱100 into ₱161. The deepest drop, on a ₱5,000 balance, was about ₱370. We chose these companies this year, so this is history, not a forecast. Take out the chip and grid-equipment makers and it's still ₱237.

And end to end: holdings reviewed every month, weights reset every quarter, about 3% in cash to pay withdrawals, no company above the BSP's 20% limit, and no type of service above 25%.

> Numbers: CAGR 24.95% → ₱305.9; NFRA ₱161; max DD −7.43% × ₱5,000 = ₱372; ex-suppliers ₱237 (`ex_suppliers.json`). Q&A backup: 36-year industry history 11.96% a year, about the world market.

## 5 · Bea's journey (5:30–7:45)

**[Chime. Notification:]**
> *BPI · ₱100 invested in Firsts Fund. Goal: First trip abroad with Mom and Dad · 2031*

Meet Bea. She's 23, a junior accountant, and takes home ₱28,000 a month. After rent, food, helping at home and her emergency savings, she has about ₱2,000 left. Her first: taking her parents to Japan, five years from now.

**[App mock-up, labelled "proposed".]**

Here's what she sees in the BPI App. She names her first and enters the cost: ₱120,000. She picks a date at least five years away. The calculator shows the plain math first: ₱2,000 a month, from contributions alone. Then an illustrative growth scenario, with every assumption on screen. If prices rise, she can switch on inflation and see the real number. And before she commits, she sees the risk in pesos, not percentages.

Four screens, and she's in. Her salary lands; her plan runs.

Then life happens. One month she's short. Nothing breaks. Her goal date stays put. The app shows her updated projection and gives her three choices: a top-up later, a little more each month, or a later date. Her choice, never automatic.

Her dashboard shows what matters: how much she's put in, what it's worth, and how close she is. No streaks. No daily price alerts.

As her date gets closer, there's less time to recover from a bad year. So every year she gets a goal review, plus a reminder two years before. If she wants, she can move part of her money to a lower-risk fund. Her account, her decision.

**[Customer roadmap slide: discover → calculator → sign up → monthly plan → yearly review → reach it → next first.]**

And when she reaches it? She names her next first, in the same account. Because there's always a new first.

## 6 · How Bea finds us (7:45–9:10)

One message: **Fund your firsts.**

**[Ad clip, ~8 s: the calculator scene.]**

We go where first-jobbers already are. On-site talks and campus caravans come first, because people trust a face. Then graduating classes and student orgs, and financial-wellness sessions for new hires, including at companies on BPI payroll. Short-form social, kept lean. And "Chat with us" for anyone online who wants to ask a person first.

Every channel ends in one action: try the calculator. Once you've typed in your first and seen ₱2,000 a month, it stops being an ad. It's a plan.

✂ What's next: Sukli, which rounds up spare change from BPI purchases into Bea's first, and more than one first at a time. Both are proposed and both get tested first.

How we'll know it works: not accounts opened, but how many people are still contributing in month 60. Our target is more than half.
