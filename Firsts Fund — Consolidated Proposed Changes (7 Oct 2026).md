# Firsts Fund — Consolidated Proposed Changes

**BPI FinQUEST | Team changes proposal | 7 October 2026**

This document consolidates the agreed revisions to the proposal and supersedes conflicting directions in the earlier changes summary. These are team decisions for a proposed product and experience, rather than claims of existing BPI functionality or product approval. Benchmark selection and revised performance analysis will be added separately.

## 1. Revised positioning

Firsts Fund is a proposed **actively managed global equity UITF**, investing directly in stocks selected through the M.V.P. framework. The number of holdings is an outcome of investment quality and portfolio construction, rather than a fixed target. Its theme centers on businesses providing essential services, infrastructure, and hard-to-replace real-world capacity. It is intended for suitable investors pursuing meaningful firsts at least five years away.

The fund has no predetermined Philippine/global allocation. Its manager selects and weights investments through M.V.P. and portfolio risk management, within applicable regulatory limits. A separate, authorized account-level glidepath would gradually move part of an investor’s goal portfolio into an appropriate lower-risk fund as their goal approaches.

**Central campaign message: “Fund your firsts.”**

## 2A. Tightened theme and M.V.P. selection process

### Theme: essential capacity with a defensible investment case

Invest in listed businesses that own, operate, or supply essential capacity supporting everyday life and economic activity, where replacing that capacity is difficult and the business can capture economic value from providing it.

Eligible activities can include energy and water systems, transport and logistics networks, communications and digital infrastructure, healthcare infrastructure, and critical industrial equipment. These examples define a research universe, not mandatory sector allocations. Technology-enabled businesses may qualify. Inclusion requires evidence of a meaningful operating exposure; a company name, sector label, or incidental business line is insufficient.

“Hard to replace” must be supported by evidence such as network scale, switching costs, scarce assets or permits, specialized capability, or the time and capital needed to replicate the service. Assess the accompanying risks: essential demand or regulatory protection does not guarantee attractive shareholder returns.

The five firsts are the customer-facing explanation of the theme. They are not exhaustive industry categories or a reason to reject a business with a sound thematic fit. A published numerical capacity target can support the assessment but is not mandatory, and an expansion announcement alone does not establish eligibility.

### M — Map the milestone: establish thematic eligibility

**Question:** What essential capacity does the company provide, why is it difficult to replace, and how does the company earn from it?

Document the relevant operations, their materiality to the business, the users served, barriers to replacement, and major competitive or regulatory risks. Then explain the connection to investors’ future firsts in plain language.

**Pass:** A material, evidenced exposure to eligible capacity and a credible mechanism for capturing economic value.

**Reject:** An incidental or unsupported theme connection, a purely promotional connection to a first, or no identifiable capacity business. Do not reject a company merely because it lacks a dated expansion target or does not fit one of the five marketing categories.

**Output:** An evidence-backed thematic eligibility note.

### V — Verify the investment: establish quality and valuation

**Question:** Is this an investable business at the current price, considering its cash generation, financial resilience, and risks?

| Assessment | Evidence to document | Grounds for rejection or deferral |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business economics | Demand durability, competitive position, pricing/regulation, returns on investment, and how growth creates shareholder value. | Growth depends on unsupported assumptions, or expansion does not have a credible economic case. |
| Cash generation and execution | Operating cash flow, maintenance and growth capital expenditure, funding needs, and execution record. | Funding needs or execution risks are not adequately supported; reported earnings do not translate into a credible cash-flow case. |
| Financial resilience | Debt maturity, interest coverage, leverage, refinancing exposure, and downside scenarios appropriate to the sector. | Debt-service or refinancing needs are not credible under reasonable adverse conditions. |
| Valuation | Sector-appropriate valuation methods, peer/history context, forward assumptions, and sensitivity analysis. | The price cannot be justified by defensible assumptions or provides inadequate compensation for identified risks. |
| Governance and evidence | Audited reports, ownership, related-party dealings, disclosures, and sources available at the assessment date. | Material governance concerns or insufficient evidence to substantiate the case. |
| Investability | Accessible listing, custody/trading feasibility, trading volume and spreads, currency exposure, and liquidity at the intended position size. | The position cannot reasonably be acquired or exited on the proposed basis. |

Remove the universal EV/EBITDA ≤22.2× cutoff and its comparison with a money-market yield. EBITDA/enterprise value is not a cash yield paid to investors. Use valuation methods suited to the business, including cash-flow analysis where appropriate.

Replace blanket debt pass/fail thresholds with documented sector-aware assessment. Quantitative ratios remain useful evidence; exceptions must be justified consistently before testing, rather than introduced to rescue preferred stocks.

**Decision:** Eligible for portfolio consideration, watchlist pending evidence or a better price, or rejected. Passing Map does not guarantee passing Verify, and passing Verify does not guarantee a portfolio position.

**Output:** A dated investment thesis, valuation case, downside assessment, and decision record.

### P — Position the risk: construct and maintain the portfolio

**Question:** What does this holding add to the portfolio, and what weight is justified?

Assess conviction and valuation alongside volatility, correlations, overlapping business exposures, issuer/group concentration, country and currency risks, liquidity, and expected trading costs. Evaluate normal and stressed conditions. Historical correlations are estimates, not guarantees of diversification.

Use risk analysis to inform active management. Equal-risk contribution may be evaluated as one candidate approach, but it is not a required meaning of M.V.P. or proof of optimal weights. Compare any proposed optimizer with simple equal-weight and other justified allocations to understand what its complexity adds.

Retain applicable regulatory limits. Remove unsupported mandatory role allocations, the one-third country quota, the fixed 10% reserve, and the custom 10% issuer ceiling from the default framework. Tighter internal limits may be introduced only with a documented risk rationale, clearly distinguished from legal requirements.

**Portfolio inclusion:** A stock must have a justified position size and add sufficient investment or diversification value after accounting for risk, overlap, and costs. A sound business may stay on the watchlist if it duplicates existing exposure or cannot be held at a sensible weight.

**Holdings count:** Add holdings when they improve the opportunity set or reduce material concentration on justified terms. Stop when further candidates fail quality/valuation assessment, mainly duplicate existing exposures, or would create negligible positions and unnecessary costs. Review whether the resulting portfolio is sufficiently diversified; a small qualifying universe is a reason to revisit the mandate or candidate search, not automatically to launch a concentrated fund. Do not force 15, 16, or any other count.

**Review:** Review the portfolio quarterly and when material events affect the thesis. This does not require trading every quarter. Increase, reduce, or exit positions when valuation, financial resilience, thematic eligibility, portfolio concentration, or investability materially changes. Record the reason for each decision.

**Output:** Portfolio weights, contribution and concentration assessment, holdings-count rationale, and documented review/exit decisions.

### Evidence and testing discipline

The recent performance deck is preliminary and does not override these revised rules. Define the selection and weighting approach before rerunning the analysis; do not change it solely to improve historical returns.

- Use only information available at each historical decision date when claiming to backtest the full M.V.P. process, including publication lags and the eligible historical universe.
- If current screens select stocks whose past returns are then tested, label the result historical performance of the currently selected portfolio. Do not claim that selection look-ahead has been removed merely because weights use lagged returns.
- Separate company-level portfolio testing from long-run industry-proxy analysis. Proxy results provide thematic context rather than validation of the exact company screens.
- Compare revised and previous rules over the same period, currency, return conventions, and realistic fee, trading-cost, dividend, cash-return, and rebalancing assumptions. A policy rate is not automatically the realized return on cash.
- Use a separate validation period or walk-forward evaluation where feasible. Report sensitivity and unfavorable outcomes alongside favorable results.
- Do not claim general downside protection, goal reliability, or a maximum possible loss from a few favorable episodes. Evaluate rolling investor contribution outcomes and the account-level glidepath separately from fund-only performance.

### Short explanation for investors

> We identify businesses providing essential capacity, check their financial strength and value, and build a portfolio that balances opportunities with risk. The number of investments follows that process.

Keep detailed evidence, rejection rules, and testing methodology in the supporting proposal. The investor fact sheet retains the simple explanation and essential disclosures.

### Complete journey

1. **Discover:** Encounter the campaign through social content, campus activities, or workplace sessions.
2. **Choose your first:** Select a suggested goal, create a custom goal, or receive help choosing.
3. **Plan:** Set an amount and date; explore affordable contributions using the calculator.
4. **Understand and authorize:** Complete suitability and setup, review disclosures, and authorize selected features.
5. **Contribute:** Start regular subscriptions and add voluntary top-ups.
6. **Monitor:** Track contributions, combined investment value, and goal progress.
7. **Review and reduce risk:** Review the goal periodically; the authorized account-level glidepath gradually adjusts the goal portfolio as the date approaches.
8. **Use or revise:** Redeem for the first, revise the plan if needed, or name the next first.

## 4. Account-level glidepath

**Decision: retain the proposed automatic account-level glidepath as a core platform concept.** It is structurally compatible with a pooled UITF because it changes the investor’s allocation across funds, rather than changing Firsts Fund’s shared mandate for each investor.

### Simple explanation

> Firsts Fund invests for long-term growth. As your chosen goal approaches, an authorized platform feature would gradually move part of your investment into an appropriate lower-risk fund, reducing exposure to equity-market fluctuations.

### Fund versus investor

| Fund level | Investor level |
| --- | --- |
| Firsts Fund follows one shared global equity mandate. | Each investor has a personal goal date and allocation across funds. |
| The manager selects and reweights stocks through M.V.P. | The proposed platform redeems part of the investor’s Firsts Fund units and reinvests the proceeds into an appropriate lower-risk fund. |
| Active management continues within the fund. | The investor’s exposure to Firsts Fund declines as their goal approaches. |

Prior authorization would cover the defined automatic mechanism. Transfers concern redemption proceeds, not only “gains,” and can occur while the investment has a gain or loss. Lower risk does not mean guaranteed capital protection or goal achievement.

Use a target-allocation approach to explain the mechanism. Remove competing 4%/5% schedules rather than presenting an unsupported precise formula. Describe the progression toward lower-risk assets without making detailed platform engineering a prerequisite for the competition concept. Any numerical schedule used in a mockup should be labeled illustrative. Treatment of new contributions should follow the intended allocation during the transition.

The dashboard should show the total value across both funds so a transfer does not appear to erase goal progress. The proposed feature should not be described as already available in the BPI App.

## Regulatory supporting note

RCBC’s official notice reproduces the amended exposure provision under BSP Circular No. 1234 (2026): UITFs invested partly or substantially in exchange-traded equities have a 20% single-issuer exposure ceiling. Exposure above 15%, up to 20%, must solely comprise that issuer’s exchange-traded equity securities. A separate benchmark-weight exception applies to equity index-tracking UITFs and is not assumed for Firsts Fund.

The earlier review could not retrieve the circular directly from BSP; the source here is an official trust-provider reproduction. The ceiling is retained as regulatory context, not as a portfolio-weight target.
